Aempaera

The heavenly realm: bright, never silent, and built inside the body of its creator.

This section follows the Aempaera and Ishphaeno reference documents. Lines without a tag are settled. Tags mark anything less settled. Names are given in Vatrahu unless a Vatraxu form is named.

At a glance

Names
Aempaera in Vatrahu and Zaempaera in Vatraxu. Decided
What it is
The celestial dominion within Jangshe. It is the home of the Vatrajarə and the seat of the Ashraama faith, which human scholars know as Abrama.
Rulers
Jangshe and Ashraaha. Jangshe is a primordial. Ashraaha was created by Jangshe and is not one.
People
The Vatrajarə. They are its soldiers, messengers and citizens, and they speak Vatrahu.
Light
Torusheva, a luminous orb that never sets.
Time
Time runs faster here than on Earth. One Earth hour is six Aempaera hours.
Seen from Earth
The stars of Aempaera are the same stars, seen from another angle.

Two primordials

PrimordialNatureRealm inside it

Ashraaha is the mother of the Vatrajarə. She is a nurturer and co-creator with queen-level standing in Aempaera, and she stays in Aethmaeri. Early documents and mortal scholars often call her a primordial, which is wrong.

Three zones

ZoneWhat it is
The Praise Fields (outer)Where the souls of mortal worshippers are ferried at death. To them it is luminous, warm and vibrant. They sing.
The Purification Passages (middle)Where souls in transit are remade as Laer, the lowest rank of the Vatrajarə.
The Vatrajarə Quarters (inner)Homes, institutions, training grounds and governing chambers. Aethmaeri is here, where Jangshe holds court and where Aethmaale, the holy weapon material, is forged. Floating platforms called Ashraethal carry the buildings, joined by roads of condensed aethaari that only a Vatrajarə can cross.

Aempaera is vast, stark and open to the air, in white stone with gold inlay. Jangshe’s court is the most architectural place. At the outer edges the buildings dissolve into boundlessness. A mortal who enters without preparation is overwhelmed by the senses within moments, and getting about without Vatrajarə power is possible but very dangerous. The praise of the outer fields is a constant low harmony that grows more complex toward Aethmaeri. Colours are stronger than mortal eyes can hold: whiter than sunlight, golds that are warm and not harsh, deep greens. A glowing cloud fills the space between buildings.

Light and the seasons

Torusheva never moves across the sky. It slowly bleaches from full brightness to a dim moonlight, then brightens again. At the dim end the stars blaze.

There are two seasons, tied to Jangshe’s own energy and not to weather. Romatoru, the alive season, is Jangshe at full capacity: the orb stays bright, aethaari refills faster, markings show more and wings glow. Hevatoru, the rest season, is Jangshe at slower capacity: the orb dims often, the stars show longer and aethaari refills more slowly. It is the natural time for meditative stillness.

Telling time

The orb does not move, so shadows always point the same way. Their length changes with the orb’s brightness, and the Shorumael, a post ringed with length-interval marks, reads it. A brighter orb gives a shorter shadow. Wind gives the finer reading: light, frequent gusts mean morning, strong long gusts afternoon, and gentler medium gusts evening. A blind or sensory-impaired Vatrajarə can tell the time by wind alone.

Earth is the neutral baseline. Ishphaeno runs slower than Earth and Aempaera runs faster.

Aethaari

Aethaari is the sacred flame of the Vatrajarə, and it is cultivated, not ambient. Singing with others refills it fastest. Meditative stillness refills it slowly, and it is one of the few reliable paths to higher rank. Using power spends it. A Vatrajarə on Earth spends it faster and recovers slower. For the Holy Princes, whose auras and True Voices cost the most, that is a daily concern.
