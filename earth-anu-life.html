Packs, orders and places

There is no Anu government. There are packs, treaties between them, and a few institutions that reach outward to humans.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Anu Canon Reference (v3). Tags mark anything less settled.

Pack life

  • Sovereignty. A pack’s rule over itself is near absolute. No global Anu authority exists. Matters between packs go to treaty, sometimes with neutral elder mediators, and outside institutions must deal with one pack at a time.
  • Gender. The society is strongly male-dominant. Female warriors are respected, but abstract leadership has long excluded women, and the first female Gammas and Betas are recent. Female Alphas are extremely rare, and a pack with one is fiercely protective of her. An Alpha without a mate and pups is customarily challenged. Inbreeding is warned against.
  • Layout. Younger Anu bunk together and elders have private dens. Rank shows in closeness to the Alpha, or is casual, depending on the pack.

  • Gestures. A shoulder clasp for loyalty and readiness, a brow press for private reassurance, a neck nuzzle for pack affection, and a silent circle before a hunt or battle.
  • Howls. Short means greeting or return. Long means alert, distress or mourning. A rising tone is victory and a falling tone is loss. A double howl alerts nearby Anu.

Dreamleaf

Daigimí is a silvery vine that grows where the Dream touches the world. It is burned at funerals and at rites of alignment to reach the ancestors. A large dose can trap a person in their thread.

Wolf Valley

The Rayan Pack holds the foothills of the Merveilles Mountains northeast of Emsehurst, north of Grey’s Lake. Human campers use the south shore, and rangers say to stay on the trail. Locals call the place Wolf Valley.

  • Concealment. Heavy canopy and mist, moss-and-grass roofs, earth-sheltered dens, low heat signatures, false trails, Dreamward runes and illusion magic. The land is legally a private hunting reserve held by a trust, and local disappearance legends do the rest.
  • Sites. A great den with a council fire and an ancestral altar, overlooking a hidden spring-fed lake called Wolf’s Mirror. A Sacred Spring for initiations, Miyema, prophecy dreams and thread burial. The Forge of ’Ògùsémün in a heated cave. A healer’s hut, an elder council hall and a communal fire pit. A training glade, an Ancestral Trail lit during the düfari rite, and a Dream-Grove for private prayer and grief.

A non-interference arrangement with the Sekh covers the area, with shared vigilance on the border.
