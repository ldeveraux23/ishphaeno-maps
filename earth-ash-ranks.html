Ranks and tools

Five ranks in a pyramid. Movement is only upward, and the marks of every earlier role stay on the body for good.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Earth reference documents. Tags mark anything less settled. Nok spellings follow the current reference documents.

The base has two entry paths and they stay separate. A Ghosthand never becomes a Witness. Everyone rises from where they began.

A mark earned on the way up is kept. A Weigher who began as a Witness keeps the owl. A Collector who began as a Ghosthand keeps the gloves. Current rank tells you what someone does, and their marks tell you where they began. Reading someone’s marks is reading their career.

The Ashborn

The council is capped at thirteen, mirroring the 13 Dream-Tethered Signs. It is not always full. When a seat opens, the Weighers nominate and the remaining Ashborn choose at their own pace. Roughly nine or ten of the current Ashborn are original river-risen members, so the faces at the top have barely changed. Twelve of the thirteen work in six pairs. The thirteenth leads, delegates and watches the whole institution, and keeps the top pair as right and left hands.

The trial of sorting

A soul arrives at the threshold in the Ways with no memory of who it was. That emptiness is the first thing the trial measures. A recruit who cannot hold together enough to move through the trial is quietly pulled out and erased. The trial has five parts, always in the same order.

StageTests forWhereWhat happens
1. DissolutionGhosthandThe WaysThe recruit meets a residue, a binding or echo caught in the Ways, and must unmake it gently, without force.
2. WitnessWitnessThe living worldThe recruit walks through a place where something already happened. They need not act. They are judged on what they saw, concluded and chose not to do.
3. CollectionCollectorThe Ways to the living worldAn active pursuit. A recruit’s first weapon appears here, not summoned, just present.
4. WeighingWeigherThe living worldA soul in real distress is put in the recruit’s path. The test is whether they can hold compassion and purpose together.
5. Deep CurrentAshbornThe river’s edgeNo instructions. The recruit stands at Kàtúngámün’s edge. Whether the river still speaks to them cannot be performed.

Weighers run the first stages and watch closely. Ashborn watch the whole, with one always hidden at Stage 5. Afterwards the Weighers hand their notes to the Ashborn, who hold a closed deliberation, much like a papal conclave, and emerge with every recruit assigned. Every new member is paired with a senior of their rank until the senior judges them ready to work alone.

Members who are sorted directly into the Ashborn have extraordinary depth and large gaps. They skipped the years of ordinary rank, and their seniors spend the pairing filling in what they missed. Canon

The nítómó weapon

Nítómó is a smooth, steely-grey river stone. The name joins nító, “between,” and mó, “stone.” It exists on both planes at once, which is why it anchors a weapon that must work on both. Everything else used in forging must also come from a river, and is best drawn from both the living world and the Ways.

  • The talisman. The weapon is anchored in a piece of jewellery worn in plain sight and touched to call the weapon. A ring is the usual form. Earrings, necklaces, bracelets and anklets are also valid, but hidden piercings are impractical.
  • The weapon. It is personal and unrepeatable. It first appears at Stage 3 of the trial, already attuned, and nobody else can hold it properly. A second, evolved form can be unlocked through experience and long reflection, though not every member reaches it.
  • The smith. One of the original river-risen Ashborn made the forging technique from nothing, using what the river gave her. All talisman jewellery passes through her, and she works blindfolded. A new member describes the design and mould, fetches the river materials, and brings them to her. Attunement follows as part of induction.

The blindfold and the gloves

Every Ashborn wears a sacred blindfold, woven by the founding weaver, who was among the first recruits and is believed to have been blind in her human life. The blindfold is Dreamleaf woven with natural silk, dyed dark grey-black with tuber extract and mud rich in nítómó ore. It layers two or three times around the head. The ore sharpens every other sense while ordinary sight is shut off, and Ashborn still see the veil with the mind’s eye. All are identical, including the smith’s and the weaver’s own. Taking it off is a serious escalation, and when an Ashborn does, something large is happening.

The same weaver made the Ghosthands’ gloves after watching the first Ashborn drain themselves opening rifts with bare hands. The old method is not lost, but only the Ashborn still use it. The weaver’s name and history are also still to be written. Placeholder

Opening and closing rifts

  • Opening a rift is easier than closing one.
  • Weapons are quicker and less precise. Bare hands are slower, more controlled and more respected.
  • Thin places near an existing weakness are easier to work than stable ground.
  • Only Ghosthands and Ashborn may open and close rifts. A Collector may open one in an emergency. Everyone else uses the sanctioned gates.

The Weigher’s palm scale

Every Weigher has a scale tattooed into both palms, received on confirmation of rank. It is an enchantment, not plain ink. Only Weighers and Ashborn can see it. When a Weigher assesses a petition, it glows cool blue to the wearer alone, and the answer is felt inside. To onlookers it looks like this: the Weigher holds out both hands palm-up, and the heavier hand sinks like a true scale. The petitioner senses a judgment being made and cannot see how.
