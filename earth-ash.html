The House of Ash

Thámaijí’Ójèpa keeps the balance between the living world, the afterlife and the Ways that lie between. Most Sekh think of it as a bogeyman. The House does nothing to correct them.

“No flame claimed them. No jackal watched them. The river simply kept them.”

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Earth reference documents. Tags mark anything less settled. Nok spellings follow the current reference documents.

At a glance

Nok name
Thámaijí’Ójèpa, “the House of Ash.”
What it does
Hunts what is out of place across the three realms, and answers petitions about cosmic imbalance for a price.
Who belongs
Human souls only. A Sekh, Anu or Lamiae cannot join.
Headquarters
In the Ways Between, in a place that is found by feel and never by map.
Loyalty
To Nyámün’s dream itself, not to any single god.

The three-part philosophy

No single rank holds the whole of the House’s work. The division is deliberate, so that no one person can misuse it.

  • Truth is witnessed. This is the work of the Wairùmín.
  • Truth is understood. This is the work of the Yírạpén.
  • Truth is acted upon. This is the work of the Paiwèpén.
  • The Ashborn, the 'Ójèpamí, hold all three, and the weight of knowing when each applies.

Four principles follow from it. Nothing is erased without leaving something behind. Truth must be seen before it is acted on. Power is divided to prevent imbalance. The marks of a member’s past roles never leave them.

The three realms

Nyámün, the All-Creator, dreams, and the dream holds everything. All three realms below are joined through the Ways.

Grey is the House’s main colour: the ash that remains when the living world’s black and the Ways’ gold are both gone. It shows in slate-grey cloaks and coveralls, and gives the House a deliberately funerary look.

The Ways Between

The Ways are not ordered and not chaotic. They are the moment before either one decides what to be. Terrain shifts, distances disagree, time runs differently in different pockets, and landmarks do not hold from one visit to the next. Experienced members navigate by feel, following the current of Kàtúngámün, the river that is the Ways’ one stable feature.

  • Kàtúngámün runs through the Ways and carries souls across all three of its tides.
  • Chaos Beasts are native to the Ways. Their crossings into the living world are a main House concern.
  • Things fall through rips in Nèbémün’s veil: people, objects, fragments of events. All of it is House business.
  • The Ways have their own ecosystem. Members eat there, and Ways food feeds them in a way living-world food does not.

Tether points

  • Divinely anchored. Places where a god’s lasting presence steadies the Ways. Nẹránamün temples stand where communities recognised an anchor and built toward it.
  • Engineered gates. Places held open by constant effort, including the House’s headquarters and dispatch gates.

The House watches the number of open rifts closely. Too many held open at once could unravel the thread of Nyámün’s dream. That is not a figure of speech, and it outranks every contract the House takes. Canon

’Anpésùmün, the Opener of Ways, keeps a strong presence in the Ways, and every sanctioned crossing passes through his doorways. The House shares the ground with him in mutual respect and no formal arrangement.

Origin

No founding document exists, and the House would never allow one. What survives is one fragment, quoted differently in three sealed archives:

“In the time when the river ran thick, when the boundary between ’Ätozo and ’Ätáru thinned to membrane, when wandering souls moved through the living world like winter through cracked walls, something rose. Not from above. Not from below. From within the water itself.”

What made the river run thick is not recorded. The institution that erases things erased its own beginning, and the House considers that appropriate. The first members did not join. They rose out of Kàtúngámün fully formed on the day of the founding event, with no past lives and no earlier ranks, already caretakers of balance. The contract work came later.

What members are

Members are human souls who answered the Third Tide. They are not alive, not dead, not undead and not reborn. They are suspended: present in the living world with full body and mind, but not woven into Daimuzónobu the way the living are.

The covenant

When a soul steps from the current onto the shore of the Third Tide, the covenant seals. Prior name, life and death are severed, and the House keeps no record of what the soul was.

  • It does not expire. There is no retirement and no release.
  • Breaking the laws ends in erasure. The Ashborn carry it out. Demotion does not exist, and every member knows it.

“The river chose. The river keeps.”
