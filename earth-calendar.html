Calendar and festivals

The Nok year has twelve named months of thirty days, followed by a five-day sacred month. It follows the Gregorian year closely.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Earth reference documents. Tags mark anything less settled. Nok spellings follow the current reference documents.

Epoch

Year 1 of the Nok count, Shòní, is 379 BCE. For dates in the common era, the Nok year is the CE year plus 379. For dates before the common era, Kishòní equals the Gregorian BCE year minus 380. So 1982 CE is 2361 Shòní.

In a Gregorian leap year, 29 February is folded into the sacred month as a sixth day, Kòbíkhèm. In those years Khèmọnai runs Jan 30 to Feb 4, and every month after it shifts up one date number but keeps its length.

No other events are scheduled on these days besides the religious ceremonies. School and work stop completely.

The week

Name days

The first and senior Sekh clan is honoured by name in the way a saint is. Each member’s day is their Initiation Day, and many Nok families choose their children’s names from them. Amadi’s own name is not used, although many names are inspired by it. The list itself is kept in the private canon.

Drift

The equinox and solstice festivals have drifted over time from their astronomical dates. Khùmäyítánéka, for example, now falls on Mar 6 rather than near Mar 20. Decided

Friday’s name, Háshùdíma, follows the settled spelling of Háshùmün, and Thursday’s name, Hóshédíma, follows its god Hóshémün the same way. Decided
