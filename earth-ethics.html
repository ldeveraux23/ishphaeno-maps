Moral framework

Five layers of law, from two sentences carved on every gate to thirty daily threads. The Anu and the Sekh each have their own wording.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Earth reference documents. Tags mark anything less settled. Nok spellings follow the current reference documents.

The moral laws of Nẹránamün are universal to all children of the Dream. The Anu hold the thread tightly, as keepers of the border and guardians of the dead. The Sekh walk it boldly, with fire in their step. Humans honour the laws in breath and memory. The Anu proverb is “Words fade. The hunt endures.” The Sekh say, “What burns is alive. What endures is wise.” The old lists of 42 and 34 Confessions are retired, and the code stays at thirty threads. Decided

How the threads are kept

  • Daily recitation. Morning and night, five threads per rotation. Five a day over a six-day Nok week covers all thirty, and a 30-day month holds five such weeks. After each, people ask themselves: “Have I done this today? Do I need to cleanse or confess?”
  • Teaching. The foundations and pillars come from toddlerhood, with survival lessons. The seven spirits come through fables, animal symbols and chants. The twelve come through mnemonic songs and scenarios. The full thirty come through call-and-response each moon, scrollwork in Nok and Common, and dream-watching journals.
  • The initiation challenge. A young Anu recites all thirty in order, explains twenty chosen at random, lives a test week judged by elder watchers, and passes a soul-mirror trial before a ’Anpésùmün priest.
  • Breaking a thread. Repeated breaks that are never repented weaken, decay or turn the spirit. A soul that dies with too many has no Shadow Walker path and wanders. Breaking one before others without confessing stains the bloodline’s name.

Strict communities

Some Anu live in devout enclaves, comparable to Hasidic communities: self-contained, ritual-precise and strict. Non-mates touch little after age 12, courtship is supervised and includes a confession of purity, and the community keeps a spiritual quarantine after contact with Lamiae blood. Fasting, silent walks and moon-watching are common. Shame in such a community carries to the whole bloodline.
