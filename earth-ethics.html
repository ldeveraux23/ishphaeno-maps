Moral framework

Five layers of law, from two sentences carved on every gate to thirty daily threads. The Anu and the Sekh each have their own wording.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Earth reference documents. Tags mark anything less settled. Nok spellings follow the current reference documents.

The moral laws of Neirạma’atámün are universal to all children of the Dream. The Anu hold the thread tightly, as keepers of the border and guardians of the dead. The Sekh walk it boldly, with fire in their step. Humans honour the laws in breath and memory. The Anu proverb is “Words fade. The hunt endures.” The Sekh say, “Ash fades. The fire endures.”

Strict communities

Some Anu live in tightly knit, devout enclaves built on purity, ritual precision and moral discipline. Children learn the Seven Truths between ages three and six through fables and chants, the Twelve Pillars between seven and ten, and the full thirty between eleven and fifteen, finishing with a recitation and a trial before the Zhawézónímís festival. Morning and evening, devout families recite five of the thirty, so the whole set is covered every six days. Breaking the code without repentance is said to weaken the animal spirit.
