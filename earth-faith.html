Nẹránamün

The faith of the Dream. It began among the Nok-speaking peoples and is the faith of the Sekh, the Anu and the human clergy who serve them.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Earth reference documents. Tags mark anything less settled. Nok spellings follow the current reference documents.

Followers

  • Shùkíneirana, the True Dreamers.
  • Daimùyùnä, anyone descended from a Nẹránamün culture, practising or not.
  • Hányídaimùyùnä, the Lost Dreamers, who have left the faith. Some call themselves Kiwásómùna, the woken ones.
  • Shùkízíngámùna, colloquially Shùkízímù, are the human devotees of ‘Èn’zíngámün. In English they are the Zingans. Decided

Core ideas

  • The Dream. Nyámün, the All-Creator, dreamed the world into being. The Daimùzónobu is the Dream-Life-Thread that runs through everything.
  • The Thread. Each life is a thread. Death is a divergence of paths, not an end.
  • Twin moons. Time’s twin faces, ‘Írubá (ebb) and ‘Írábùkh (flow), made two moons, Khùzú and Khùzí. They later crashed together into a single scarred moon, Khùm, in the event called Dímakhùm Daimùzónobu. On Earth this is a story carried over from Kanna and passed down for centuries, so it is an intentional distortion of the original moons. Decided Khùm still carries both legacies: waxing, it remembers Khùzí’s fire, and waning, it whispers Khùzú’s memory. If Khùm ever splits again, the thread will fray and the age of balance will end.
  • Two worlds, one veil. ‘Ätozo is the living world and ‘Ätáru the afterlife. Nèbémün keeps the Veil between them, Kàtúngámün’s river carries souls across, and Wạdíyèmün the Serpent Guardian protects the crossing.
