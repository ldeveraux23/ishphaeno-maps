Rebirth and the Four Trials

A human does not become a Sekh by wanting it. They die in fire, walk the Dream alone, and either return or dissolve.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Earth reference documents. Tags mark anything less settled. Nok spellings follow the current reference documents.

Who is chosen

Becoming a Sekh is closer to sainthood than promotion. Most acolytes (Sèkhèt’mùna) serve the faith for their whole human lives, and Rebirth is a rare grace, not an expectation. When someone is chosen, the community holds a funeral on the scale of a saint’s canonisation, with celebration and mourning together. The human is honoured. Contact with family usually ends, because the bloodlust that follows makes proximity dangerous.

Four phases

PhaseNok nameWhat happens
1. The BurningNg’nairèt’mün’éhThe Initiator (‘Äkumukhèt) draws their own blood and traces ‘Èn’zíngámün’s sigil, Jaibüng’éh, on the initiate’s chest. The blood ignites without fuel and consumes the mortal body. The initiate enters the Dream as the body burns. The Ikh’shímùyùnéh, the Flame-Blood Blade, was used only for the first three attempts and belongs to Amadi alone.
2. The Dream ThreadNígbamonatùn’éhThe initiate walks the Four Trials alone.
3. The Divine ThresholdNeirậpúwózím’éhIf all four are passed, ‘Èn’zíngámün appears, flame-clad, and offers her blessing.
4. AwakeningNg’wásórèt’mün’éhThe fire dies. The body is revealed, healed and marked. The Initiator speaks the new name.

Victory is a balance of breath, flame, self and instinct. Failure is an imbalance in the Dream.

Failure

A soul that fails is not punished or devoured. It dissolves into the Daimùzónobu, an untethered thread that is no longer a self. The Ghosthands of the House of Ash later find these threads and finish their dissolution, returning them to the fabric of the Dream. The Nok name for failed Rebirth is Kikíwàrï’khámé. This is not judgment.

Blessing and naming

Every successful initiate meets ‘Èn’zíngámün at the end of the Dream, and she gives the same blessing to all:

“Child of my flame, you have walked the Dream and returned. Let your breath carry fire, your shadow walk true. Rise, new child of Sekh.”

She named only the founding Zhèmuno clan herself. Everyone since is named by their Initiator or a senior clan member:

“By the fire you carry and the thread you walk, I name you [Name]. Walk well among the Sekh, and let your thread burn bright.”

Some acolytes prepare a name in advance, though tradition says to wait until after surviving. To awaken nameless is to walk unfinished. On rare occasions of destiny, the goddess whispers a name into the Dream once more.

After the fire

  • No shadow. Humans cast shadows because they walk beside the Daimùzónobu as a separate being. A Sekh internalises their shadow, the Khaifùya, during the last trial, so the body casts none. The Anu call the Sekh “Thread-runners.”
  • The mark. The Khùnaimímaké appears on the skin, and the Khùzí shows as a living tattoo.
  • Bloodlust. About a decade of danger to humans. The Initiator and the clan become the Sekh’s whole world during it.

Open questions
