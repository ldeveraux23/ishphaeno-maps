The thread tradition

Every mature Anu wears a thread: a visible sign that they walk the Dream, and a way to show who they are walking it with.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Earth reference documents. Tags mark anything less settled. Nok spellings follow the current reference documents.

The Nok name for it, Dúmùkwò, loosely “thread binding,” is a working name and may change. Proposed Everything on this page is Proposed until confirmed.

Wearing a thread

A mature Anu wears a thread around the hair, braided in, woven into a loc or tucked behind the ear, or around the neck as a cord or talisman. Each is a little different in colour, weave and material, because each is a spirit. It is private but visible, and it says: “I am a walker of the Dream.”

Through a life

StageThreadMeaning
Childhood and trainingNone, or a tiny plain oneStill learning.
AdulthoodA personal thread at hair or neckWalking the Dream independently.
CourtingA small piece of the other’s thread braided into your ownA soft promise that is easily undone without shame.
MatingCores exchanged, or a new thread woven from bothTwo life paths permanently joined.
MourningA broken thread tied to the wristHonouring loss until the mourning period ends.
Letting goThe broken thread buried at a sacred springReturning the dead to the Hunt.

After a formal bond the original thread is kept in a secondary place, such as a wrist or near the heart. Some traditional Anu burn the old thread at the ceremony and weave a new one together. Loose threads mean unmated. Woven or shared threads mean bonded. Frayed or snapped threads mean death, betrayal or deep mourning.

Spoken phrases

Burying a broken thread“I release your thread to the Hunt.”
Swearing brotherhood, not romantic“Two threads. One hunt.”

Private break“Your thread frays. I walk without you.”

The severance

In rare, tragic cases of betrayal, murder or abandonment, the wronged Anu cuts or burns the thread of the other before witnesses at the central fire or sacred spring. The thread is not buried with honour. It is thrown into a wild fire or onto black stone. Being cut off is shameful for the one cut, not for the one who cuts. Severed threads are sometimes kept in clan memory songs as a warning: “Remember ____, whose thread was burned.” The pack may still honour the soul in the Dream but no longer protects the name with warmth.
