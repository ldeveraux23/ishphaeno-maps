The Orendanansi

Akan captives, brought illegally to America in 1810, escaped to the Mohawk Nation in 1824. After two years they fell in love and, in 1827, founded their own tribe in upstate New York. After the Fugitive Slave Act of 1850 they moved to West Virginia, in 1852. They worship Anansi and Orenda. Before any contract they had animal-strength gifts, in short bursts or from birth: monkey-like agility, cheetah speed, rhino-like skin.

The First Night of Mefi, 1851

Three elders led a seven-night ritual, two as officiants and one as scribe. Everyone gave blood. On the seventh night a figure of spider and man appeared: Mefi, Keeper of Contracts. In exchange for beast-shifting he set the price, a spirit burden, and the renewal every ten generations. The blood rose like threads, blackened and pooled. The elders signed with a bone stylus. Anyone over 19 at the time became Gen 1, and anyone under 19 became Gen 2. Decided

Names

  • The Orendanansi name persists in ritual and in the patron’s records.
  • After the Snyder Act of 1924 the family took English surnames: Greenwood, Highwood, Ironwood, Lightwood, Songwood, Redwood, Windwood, Stormwood and Hearthwood. The marriages of the 1850s carry these names retroactively.
  • Male children take the more prestigious parent’s name. Female children take their father’s.
  • The clan leader is voted in at each renewal, usually from the Greenwoods or the Highwoods.
  • The surname rule does not say what happens in a same-sex couple Open

The Cooper stability scale

An object’s stability when teleported falls as it matters more to someone: functional objects are stable. Then come owned and replaceable objects, gifted objects, identity objects, heirlooms, sacred or contract-tied objects, and finally permanence objects. Frequent teleporting destabilises an object, and the Spatial Fixation Disorder is worst in the earliest generations. Sacred and contract-tied objects, including Sekh, Anu and Lamiae artifacts, draw magical attention.
