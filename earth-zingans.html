Zingans

Human devotees of ’Èn’zíngámün, the flame and storm daughter of Nyámün. They live close to the Sekh, and some of them hope to become Sekh.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Earth reference documents. Tags mark anything less settled. Nok spellings follow the current reference documents.

At a glance

English name
Zingans. “Sekh” stays as the one holdover from the old name. Decided
Nok name
Shùkízíngámùna, in speech Shùkízímù, “followers of ’Èn’zíngámün.”
Wider faith
They belong to the Shùkíneirana, the True Dreamers, which includes every follower of Nẹránamün. Anyone descended from a Nẹránamün culture, practising or not, is a Daimùyùnä. Those who leave are Lost Dreamers, Hányídaimùyùnä, and some call themselves Kiwásómùna, the woken ones.
Ascetic sect
The Ikhùkímùna, the Flame Followers, also called the Red Choir or the Choir of Red Dust: ritual dancers, oral keepers, blood-rite singers and temple guardians. They use red ochre and burnt cloth, and keep the tales of Nzinga’s fire. Its devout monastic branch is the House of Ember. Both follow the thirty threads.
Symbol
The Symbol of the Faithful, Jaibütùw’éh: the divine flame circle with an eye at its centre. Zingans and Sekh both wear it, and the Sekh prefer not to be conspicuous.
Place in the Sekh tier system
At the base, below formal Sekh acolytes (Sèkhèt’mùna), who are human devotees in training or in the service of the faith. Zingans are recognised as members of the faith and protected in Sekh territory.
Abilities
None innate, and none gained from worship. They do learn Makésho, the sacred arts, which are open to Zingans and Sekh alike. A lifelong devotee can outdo an untrained Sekh. Decided

The sacred arts they practise

  • Fùmamaké, sacred ritual tattooing.
  • Daigímíwaḳùn, Dreamleaf trance and veil-thinning.
  • Nímawaḳùn, ritual healing, herb-lore and thread-reading.
  • Daimùwaḳùn, counsel through the 13 Dream-Tethered Signs.
  • Ikhùwaḳùn, performance as ritual: chant and blood rite, done by the Red Choir.

Each is described on Sacred arts and symbols.

In Emsehurst

Most Zingans live in the Zingan Quarter, east of the Squannock River in the Merveilles Valley. The district has had this name in every source, including the 1933 Pact. Decided

  • Yírímbo Zonémi is their spiritual and teaching centre, founded in 1738 in Hurste. It is the oldest Nẹránamün institution still working in the Americas.
  • Moreau College (Móró Khọzìyí) is the college where the faith’s two groups meet: devotees who have studied for decades, and the newly Reborn. It is one of five institutions of its kind in the world. Many people compete to teach there, to work at The Meridian Group, to become acolytes, and in the end to become Sekh.

The hope of Rebirth

The Zingan aspiration is Rebirth: becoming a Sekh. The community speaks of it openly, and the Sekh regard it with mixed feelings. Not every practitioner fully believes in it. It is nearer to sainthood or nirvana than to a promotion. A human who goes through Rebirth does not come back, because something else returns. Contact with family is usually cut, because the bloodlust that follows makes proximity dangerous. Those chosen receive a funeral on the scale of a saint’s canonisation, with celebration and mourning together, and families may later make offerings to them.

The Sekh do not encourage the hope and do not forbid it. Only a Sekh at least a hundred years past their own Rebirth may Initiate, and there are only about 100 to 150 Sekh in the world. Most Zingans never act on it. A very few do. See Rebirth and the Four Trials.

The Anu-side parallel

The human devotees of ’Anpésùmün as a faith group, the Shùkí’Apésùmù or Shùkapémù, stand to the Anu as the Zingans do to the Sekh. The Order of Shadows is their organised order. See Packs, orders and places. Placeholder
