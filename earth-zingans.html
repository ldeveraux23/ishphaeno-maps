Zingans

Human devotees of ’Èn’zíngámün, the flame and storm daughter of Nyámün. They live close to the Sekh and hope, some of them, to become Sekh.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Earth reference documents. Tags mark anything less settled. Nok spellings follow the current reference documents.

At a glance

English name
Zingans. Decided
Nok name
Shùkízíngámùna, in speech Shùkízímù.
Sect
The Ikhùkímùna, the Red Choir, is their ascetic order.
Wider faith
They are part of the Shùkíneirana, the True Dreamers, which includes every follower of Nẹránamün.
Place in the Sekh tier system
At the base, below formal Sekh acolytes (Sèkhèt’mùna). They are recognised as members of the faith and protected in Sekh territory.
Abilities
None from worship. They gain community, purpose and closeness to beings who have already become what they hope to be.

In Emsehurst

Most Zingans live in the Zingan Quarter, east of the Squannock River in the Merveilles Valley, which runs up into the mountains. Open

  • Yírimbo Zonémi is their spiritual and teaching centre, founded in 1725 in the Old Temple Quarter. It is the oldest Nẹránamün institution still working in the Americas.
  • Moreau College is one of the Zingan institutions in the world. It began as a seminary school, and its students, staff and culture are strongly Zingan. Many students arrive already practising. Others meet the faith there for the first time because the Quarter is next door.

The hope of Rebirth

The Zingan aspiration is Rebirth: becoming a Sekh. The community speaks of it openly, and the Sekh regard it with mixed feelings. Not every practitioner fully believes in it. It is nearer to sainthood or nirvana than to a promotion. A human who goes through Rebirth does not come back, because something else returns. Contact with family is usually cut, though many people revere those believed to have been reborn.

The Sekh do not encourage the hope and do not forbid it. Most Zingans never act on it. A very few do. See Rebirth and the Four Trials.
