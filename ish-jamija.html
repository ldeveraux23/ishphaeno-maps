The Jamija

The four mates of Sadok, and the wardens of Ishphaeno’s gates.

This section follows the Ishphaeno Governance Compendium and the Aempaera references. Lines without a tag are settled. Tags mark anything less settled. Names are given in Vatraxu unless a Vatrahu form is named. It is written to be read before the books, so some family detail is left out on purpose.

At a glance

Name
Jamija (many) and Jami (one). Their title is Jami. Decided
Who
Four women: Hashaeqa, Naevoshi, Pholaeth and Zaelith. They are the original mates of Sadok.
Species
Vatrajaxo, counted as the first of them. They were changed alongside Sadok at the Fall rather than falling themselves. See The Vatrajaxo. Decided
Role
Gate wardens and regional administrators. They are not commanders over the princes. Their authority runs parallel.
Sons
Hashaeqa bore Mephithoveles, Belzevuv and Zamodes. Naevoshi bore Mamon and Zarazael, Pholaeth bore Lamjamon and Dagan, and Zaelith bore Belishael. See The Lamqaxath Monja.
Descendants
The Jamira, the succubi of Ishphaeno, descend from them. The Vashaanil, the incubi, arose later.
Dress
The Xashvael, a wrap of twelve to fourteen passes sealed with sovoq. See Clothing and Regalia.

Each gate is held by one of the four and their estates sit near their gates. See Places and Powers. Decided

How they live

The princes were raised collectively and each Jami held authority over every child. The four are not rivals. Their relationship is dry camaraderie: practicality, ancient familiarity, institutional trust, blunt honesty, mutual competence and shared trauma. Love between them is measured by endurance, not tenderness. Decided

Every child born to the Jamija after the first eight sons has been a daughter. Those daughters are the first of the Jamira. Decided
