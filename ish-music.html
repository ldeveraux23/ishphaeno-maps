Instruments and Music

Ishphaeno makes music from what it already has: skulls, wing-bone, living crystal and a wetland flower that happens to be shaped like a horn.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Ishphaeno musical instruments and ensembles reference. Materials are explained on Materials and Craft.

Skull drums and percussion

InstrumentKindDescription
MresotharuSkull-banjoA polished Mresorak head carapace as the body, sometimes back-stretched with thin Mresovel. One eye socket holds a single drone string and the other is a grip. Three melody strings run between the mandibles, which double as the bridge. Folk versions use twisted Kelnith cord, court versions use Luthorun silk-thread, and a strange high-end version uses living Sobraevael tendrils that keep growing, so it needs retuning from growth, not wear. Humble and borderland-coded, the music of the Mresibooshle folk.
RuqorathumBass drumA hollowed Ruqorith skull with a Ruqorith-hide membrane. Deep and communal, the standard harvest-festival drum and the working drum of Ruqoravael.
VulqorathumWar drumThe same build on a Vulqorith mount skull, taken only from an animal that died of old age, since these are bred and bonded, not hunted. Enormous and deep, played before battle in Lamjamon’s territory.
DronathumHunter’s drumA rarer, sharper-toned drum from a wild Droneth skull. Higher and more piercing than herd-stock drums. Owning one is a hunter’s trophy.
QorashilQuill tambourineA Ruqorith horn or hide-wrapped wood hoop strung with paired Qorazil quills in place of metal zils. Makers select and position quills by pitch, so the tone is craft-tunable. A thin Mresovel or hide head turns it into a frame-drum hybrid.

Strings

InstrumentKindDescription
SobrathelLiving-string instrumentSobraevael tendrils across a Qalthorut-wood frame. Because the tendrils keep faintly growing, pitch drifts during one session, and mastery means chasing a string that will not hold still.
VorunathelHarpStrung across a Vorunith wing-bone frame with the feathers left on. They vibrate in sympathy with the plucked strings and add a soft shimmering overtone.
ZelnithukPanpipesBound from hollow shed limb casings of molted Zelnith and lashed with Kelnith cord. The cheapest, most universal folk instrument, because the material is a byproduct of an industry already running at bread-production scale.

Vibrants

InstrumentKindDescription
SobraevrinCrystal chimesTuned Sobraeqal shards struck like chimes. Each strike gives a faint glow, and during a Toluqavel display the chimes pulse roughly in sync with the aurora. Rare, sourced from TeDrona, almost always ceremonial.
SovoxelGong or barsForged, tuned sovoq. A deep, martial, carrying tone and Lamjamon’s court instrument of choice. The same pattern used to open a ceremonial piece is legible across a garrison as an alarm.
VitharinGlass-tone bowed membraneSobraevit wing membrane stretched over a small frame and played by bow, breath or fingertip friction. An eerie, glassy, near-continuous tone like a bowed water-glass, associated with Belishael’s dream and astral ceremonies.
ZelivrithumLight drumA drumhead embedded with cultured strips of bioluminescent Zelivrrutes eel tissue, flashing faintly with each strike. Visible underwater or in cave darkness, so it doubles as a signal drum.

Winds

InstrumentKindDescription
RuqorivelBone fluteCarved from Ruqorith long-bone. Cheap and common, and the first instrument most Ishphaenan children learn.
XathelothivelDeep bone fluteCarved from Xathelun bone. Lower and breathier than the common flute, and owning one carries a hunter’s prestige.
SobraevoiCrystal hornHollow, naturally tubular Sobraeqal played like a horn. The warmth of breath makes it faintly glow while sounding. Rare and ceremonial.
VorunivethTransverse fluteVorunith wing-bone, naturally hollow and light, with several finger holes for real melodic range. Feather fringe at the mouth end adds a breathy shimmer.
ZelnitheraReed pipesShort hollow segments of molted Zelnith casing, lashed in graduated lengths. A double-reed buzzing tone, and the cheapest wind instrument in Ishphaeno.
XathvorethConch hornA conch-style horn from a large Xatheloru shell. Deep and carrying, and the traditional signal sounded from the docks of Qaeldreth and Qaelvaelvo for ship arrivals and departures.

The brass family

Vondrathovun grows in shallow water and riverbank mud along both the Malovrrutes and the Zelivrrutes. Its long flower bulb is already shaped like a horn bore and bell, narrow at the stem and flaring at peak bloom. Cut at peak bloom and dried, the fibrous walls stiffen to something like bark, hollow and resonant. Blown as harvested, it gives a deep, sustained, trombone-like tone with no shaping at all. Trimming the flared head and tightening the taper by hand before it hardens brightens the tone toward a trumpet. The two voices are finishing choices on the same bulb, and a skilled maker knows exactly how far to trim a given bulb to reach the tone a musician wants. The trombone is the plant’s default voice, and the trumpet is the one that takes craft, which inverts Earth’s usual brass hierarchy. The finished instrument is the Vondrathoreth.

Krathovun is a tall, palm-like tree along the streams and pools at the foot of the West’s floating-island waterfalls, exactly where prey gathers. Fine fibres sensitive to motion run its height. When warm-blooded prey passes close, the crown whips violently and flings its hardened fruit with force. The fruit, Krathoq, is coconut-shaped but far denser, dense enough to crack bone, and it still stores water inside. Once prey is down, the roots sense blood in the soil, grow toward it and digest the remains from below. In dry periods the feeding roots recede to the taproot, so the danger zone reads like a flood line, wide in the wet season and narrow in the dry. The strike itself is dangerous in any season.

A split and hollowed Krathoq shell becomes the Krathvreth, a mute cupped over a Vondrathoreth’s bell. It tightens the tone toward something nasal and urgent, with a slight buzz from the fibrous interior. One Vondrathoreth therefore has three registers: natural bulb, trimmed bulb, and either one muted.

Ensembles

EnsembleOccasionMake-up
Vaetheluqavrin, the sky-bound ceremonial arrayThe most formal court occasions at Xathqaelsel, and the most solemn seasonal moments.A tuned array of Sobraevrin chimes whose glow-and-ring responds to the aurora in patterned sequence, so the sky plays part of the piece. Trained musicians add Sovoxel strikes, the Vitharin drone and the drifting Sobrathel strings around that core. It works only under open sky, so the most solemn halls are open courtyards or have an oculus, closer to a Pantheon than an enclosed throne room. Indoors the crystals hold only a dim afterglow. No two performances are alike.
Zelnithuvrek, the harvest ensembleStruck up the moment a Zelnith field finishes molting and the grain-flour harvest begins.Zelnithuk panpipes and Zelnithera reed pipes carry the melody, Qorashil drives rhythm and a Mresotharu holds a low drone, with the tempo climbing. Loud and deliberately imperfect, played by whoever grabs an instrument.
Shaelovrin, the formal court ensembleThe standing formal arrangement for any court occasion, joyous or ceremonial.Vorunathel and Voruniveth form the melodic core. Xathelothivel enters underneath as a countermelody. Sobraevoi punctuates entrances, toasts and declarations. Vitharin sustains a continuous drone beneath everything. Sovoxel anchors the rhythm and marks transitions. Vondrathoreth supplies the only brass voice.

Three registers, three occasions. The Vaetheluqavrin is unpredictable and sky-dependent. The Zelnithuvrek is loud and harvest-triggered. The Shaelovrin sits between them as the dependable formal default.
