The Calamity

A million quiet years, then a few thousand loud ones. The Vorfae drained the field that held their sky together, and the moons collided.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Kanna reference documents. Tags mark anything less settled.

The timeline

PhaseSpanWhat happens
1. The Silent DriftAbout 1,000,000 Earth years (about 1.7 million Kanna years, roughly 3,100 Vorfae generations)Gate-working draws down the ley field. Kiravin's eccentricity creeps up from 0.25, far too slowly for anyone to notice in a lifetime.
2. The ThresholdNot datedEccentricity passes 0.266 and close encounters begin. At 0.364 the two orbits physically cross.
3. The Visible CollapseAbout 9,000 Earth years (about 15,500 Kanna years, roughly 28 generations)The sky misbehaves within living memory. The moons stop keeping their calendar, tides run wrong, and the wandering festival drifts out of recognition. This is the phase people witness.
4. ImpactOne dayKiruvyn and Kiravin collide. Most debris stays in orbit and gathers again. A fragment about 18 km across strikes Kanna at about 13 km/s, with about twice the energy of the Chicxulub impact on Earth.
5. AftermathDecades to centuries and beyondImpact winter, a global firestorm worsened by 30% oxygen, extreme tides, centuries of meteors, and a visible debris ring that takes millions of years to clear.

The impact is civilization-ending but survivable for sheltered and marine populations, which is exactly the survival pattern the peoples show. Canon

The myth and the mechanism

The Vorfae tell it as dying forests and a sickening sky. Kresyva felt it first, and sacrificed themself to hold Kanna steady long enough for the convoys to escape. The mechanical account says the same thing in numbers: the trees sickened because the ley field was draining, and Kresyva's sacrifice reads as the last surge of that field, spent to buy the Exodus time. The two accounts sit side by side rather than in tension. Canon More on the Vorfae page.

Sidlosa, the moon that remains

Sidlosa (also Khùm) formed from the merger. It has 0.38 Luna's mass, orbits at 313 Mm in 21.39 days, and looks about 0.38° across, visibly smaller than Kiruvyn once was. Its tides are steady and weaker, at 0.70 times Luna. Canon

The calendar after

  • The new sky holds 8.886 moon-months per year, not a whole number. The old year held exactly 12.00, and no adjustment can make the old calendar fit the new sky.
  • Every descendant culture still keeps the 12-month year, out of reverence for a sky none of them has ever seen. They count twelve months while the moon above them keeps a different rhythm.
  • The tides broke too. Before, two moons gave an irregular combined range of 0.71 to 0.98 times Luna. Now one moon gives a steady 0.70. Every creature that timed its breeding to the two-moon beat lost its cue for good, a mass extinction in the intertidal zone separate from the impact.

A hotter, burnier world

  • The greenhouse rebound pushed the equatorial heat higher. The uninhabitable band now reaches about ±18°.
  • With mature forest destroyed and replaced by fast post-fire growth in 30% oxygen, the fire season is more frequent and more severe than before, and has not fully recovered.

Who survived

  • Anything keyed to the two-moon tides is extinct, for reasons unrelated to the impact.
  • Plants with underground storage organs and fire-cued seeds were already suited to what the Calamity produced, and now dominate.
  • Riverside corridor species survived disproportionately. Expect relict species found nowhere else.
  • Fast post-fire plants inherited a planet with no competition, and the modern landscape is still shaped by that head start.
  • Color did not change. Hvan is still orange, so the biosphere is still dark red, bronze and purple-black.
