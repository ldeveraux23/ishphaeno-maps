  • Kanna sits only 0.025 AU outside the moist-greenhouse limit. It hugs the inner edge of its habitable zone rather than sitting comfortably in the middle.
  • A bare rock at that distance would sit near −21°C. About 50°C of greenhouse warming lifts it to the mean, roughly 1.5 times Earth's, which fits 20 to 30 times Earth's carbon dioxide plus extra water vapor.

Ley damping, the sky's stabilizer

  • Kanna's ley field acts as a damping medium that keeps the moons' orbits from growing more eccentric. It is a magical amplification of real tidal damping, so no one has to change gravity.
  • Ley is a finite reservoir that refills at a natural rate. The Vorfae were the most ley-active civilization the planet produced, through everyday construct-work and above all through opening and sustaining gates to other worlds.
  • Over hundreds of thousands of years that drew the field below the level needed to hold the moons steady. The Vorfae destabilized their own sky through the ordinary running of a magical civilization that had no way to know what it was spending.

Kiravin visibly breathes across her cycle. Kiruvyn never changes. The theological split between Order and Chaos is not symbolic: it is what anyone can see by looking up. The pair are held in place by ley damping, not by geometry. Canon

The calendar, before the Calamity

  • A year was exactly 192 local days: 12 months of 16 days, each month one full Kiruvyn phase cycle, with no drift.
  • Four seasons of 48 local days each.
  • Kiravin's cycle is 34.9 local days and deliberately does not divide the year. That produced a wandering ritual calendar running beside the fixed solar one.

Climate and seasons

  • The tropics reach ±26° and the polar circles ±64°, so the polar regions are much larger than Earth's. At the poles day and night can each last up to 96 local days.
  • Seasons turn about 1.7 times faster than Earth's.

The Burnt Belt

  • The Belt covers 0 to 18° in both hemispheres and touches inhabited land directly on each side.
  • It is not a desert. Lethal wet-bulb heat needs humidity, so the equator is a steaming rainforest under a permanent cloud band. The true deserts sit at 30 to 38°, where dry air descends.
  • Coasts and island chains stay passable because the ocean softens the heat. The continental interior does not.

Fire as a season

At 30% oxygen, fire catches in damp fuel and spreads almost unchecked. Above about 25% wildfire becomes near-continuous, and 35% is the usual ceiling before forests cannot survive at all. Fire is as regular and expected as winter, and every land ecosystem is built around surviving it. Canon

The living world

  • The biosphere is dark. With little blue light, photosynthesis broadens into red and infrared, so foliage runs deep red, bronze, maroon, rust and purple-black. A green plant would be a conspicuous oddity.
  • Fire adaptation is mandatory: thick insulating bark, underground storage organs that resprout within days, seeds that need heat or smoke to wake, wet riverside refuges, and fast post-fire opportunists.
  • Many plants carry two leaf forms. Small, thick, dark leaves handle the summer. Larger, thinner leaves catch the weak winter light. The same forest looks very different between seasons.
  • The coasts follow two moons. Kiravin's changing distance made tides irregular, which allowed huge intertidal forests, salt-tolerant reedlands, and creatures that spawn only on rare deep-conjunction tides.
  • Boskamyra: the armored cat, jaguar-sized with pangolin scales, living on the coastal jungle fringe. Once a trophy hunt, now the Dorrwfø rite of passage.
