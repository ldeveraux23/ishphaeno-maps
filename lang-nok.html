Nọk

The language of the Sekh and the Anu, and the language the Nok faith calls the tongue of the gods.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Kanna reference documents. Tags mark anything less settled.

Sounds and spelling

Several sounds are written as letter pairs. Each pair counts as one letter in the alphabet.

LettersSound
khVoiceless velar fricative, like the ch in Scottish loch
chVoiceless uvular fricative, a deeper sound at the back of the throat
nyPalatal nasal, like the ñ in Spanish
kwA k followed by a w
zhVoiced postalveolar fricative, like the s in measure
shVoiceless postalveolar fricative, as in English
ngVelar nasal, as in sing

Nọk has ten vowels and four diphthongs: ai, ei, oi and au. Nasal vowels are written with an umlaut. An acute or grave accent changes the quality of a vowel, and a dot under a vowel shortens it.

Dialects

Nọk has one standard form and three regional dialects. Each dialect adds a few letters or blends of its own.

DialectNọk nameWhere and whenWhat it sounds likeExtra letters and blends
StandardZaukumùFormalized and taught from 500 CE in Sekh-funded schools and Sekhmetian seminaries.A textbook form, like standard Mandarin. Educated speakers can all use it, and uneducated speakers can roughly follow it.None beyond the base alphabet
NorthernTaucùmùNorth America. Documented from 1869, though speakers were there earlier. The youngest dialect and the emerging common tongue of Nọk speakers there.Anglicisms, the letter c for /k/, slower drawled syllables, and more abbreviated speech.nd, br, st, nk, and c at the start of a word
WesternNasibumùThe coastal cities of Thímùjạnùrèt, from modern Senegal to Angola.More glottal stops, many d and t sounds corrupted to z, and a clear l at the start of a syllable.mbg, mb, and l
SouthernRanakumùngSouth America, especially Brazil. Large numbers of Nọk speakers arrived in the 1300s.Vowels and intonation colored by Portuguese, and a nasal ng that punctuates sentences.ts, tsh, nd, hw, mb

Shared blends in every dialect are kw, gw, gb, ngw, nw and mw.
