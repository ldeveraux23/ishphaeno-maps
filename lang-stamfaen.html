The Stamfaen family

The Stamfaen family: one ancestral Vorfae tongue and the daughters it left on Earth and on Kanna.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Kanna reference documents. Tags mark anything less settled.

Where Stamfaen comes from

Stamfaen fuses Old Norse and Danish sound patterns with Bantu roots, mainly from Lingala and Kikongo. It always keeps its full ae, never the circumflex. Canon

Spelling conventions

  • è (e grave) is a stressed /ɛ/.
  • ê (circumflex) marks the ae to ê reduction in the daughter languages. Stamfaen always keeps the full ae.
  • Names of peoples carry the ê: Lyndfê̂, Rejfê, Kannafê, Sharafê, Dorufê̂ (the Dorrwfø call themselves Dorrwfø) and Muldfê̂ (the Mylzhfã call themselves Mylzhfã). The Vorfae name stays unreduced.
  • Mylzhfã and Dorrwfø each have a plain-text spelling for typing and search.
