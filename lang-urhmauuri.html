Urhmauuri

The language of the deep-ocean Urhmaiia, a language of five politeness levels and sounds made for water.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Kanna reference documents. Tags mark anything less settled.

This page is in progress. Dialects are on their own branch below.

At a glance

PropertyDetail
NamesUrhmauuri in keyboard spelling and Uʁmaʉɾi in the formal spelling. The people call themselves Urhmauua. Outsiders say Urhmaiia.
Word orderSubject, object, verb
StructureAgglutinative. Suffixes carry tense, case, aspect and politeness.
PluralsWords do not change for number. Context shows it.
VerbsNo conjugation. Verbs keep one root and take only a politeness ending.
VowelsVowel harmony, matching vowels by backness, height and roundness
NounsSuffixes mark case, possession and location. Adjectives take the same cases.
ScriptUrhtsorna, an abugida with a wave crown stroke. See Writing systems.

Sounds

There is no w, v or q. Syllables follow the shapes CV, VCV, CVV and V, and the only consonant allowed to close a syllable is rh. The language has two valid ways to write certain sounds: the formal spelling uses the real IPA symbol, and the keyboard spelling uses a simple stand-in such as rh for ʁ. Neither is more correct.

The five politeness levels

The ending of the main or final verb sets the level for the whole sentence. A speaker who opens in one level carries it to the end.

LevelVerb endingUsed for
Plain polite-oStandard address in most formal or polite situations
InformalnoneFriends, peers and equals
Intimate-eA mate or life partner only
Respectful-iSomeone of higher status or age
Royal-uThose who hold royal titles only

Pitch backs up the level. Plain and informal speech ends flat or gently falling, the intimate level lowers and softens, and ritual speech uses a slow, low fall. Do not confuse these levels with the six religious-name registers, which govern which name is used for the creator and not how politely someone is addressed. See Gods and faith.

An example

LevelSentenceMeaning
Plain politeNaʉa leruʁa maiteo anetaoI like to walk on the shore.
RespectfulNaʉa leruʁa maitei anetaiI like to walk on the shore.
InformalNaʉa leruʁa maite anetaI like to walk on the shore.

Word building

The agent suffix -ii turns a thing or action into a person tied to it. Zoruii, a vent-brewer, comes from zoru, vent or pressure. Two near-synonyms can split by scope: Lera is the earth as a planet, a force or nature, while zekha is the earth as dirt and ground underfoot.
