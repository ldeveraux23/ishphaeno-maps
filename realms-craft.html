Urhmaiia

Urhmaiia dress for adornment, not cover, because loose fabric is no use in the water. Garment names and the universal loincloth are on Appearance and dress. The materials behind them are below.

Gold (Azoilira) is reserved for the Ajoiru royal line and ceremonial stewards and is never functional, because gold is too soft. Most everyday clothing is biodegradable. Treated leather, reforged metal and status materials are the deliberate exceptions, kept and passed down because permanence is rare. Iron comes from the shed scales of the scaly-foot gastropod (Kohusoa).

Music and sound

The Urhmaiia’s music is voice. Haulorai, the uvular call speech, is “deep song” or “abyss voice,” used for long-distance communication, mourning calls, mate calls and soothing animals. Its full inventory includes clicks and whistles absent from ordinary speech. The siren call to the water is Goria, and the death or loss call is Zorugoria. At a hatching, the whole community gathers at the Raunutua and contributes its Haulorai, and the vibrations physically help guide the hatchling out. The oldest sacred Haulorai compositions and mourning calls keep an archaic consonant-cluster onset that is no longer a living feature. See the Urhmaiia language page and life and rites.

Earth

  • Sekh shifting and clothing: clothes are not part of the soul and do not travel through a shift by default. Old Sekh can extend their soul’s definition of self to include clothing with training and concentration, but an emergency shift strips even ancient Sekh back to soul-claimed items. Most keep spare clothing in cars, lockers and offices. See The Sekh.
  • Nẹránamün practice uses a slow rhythm of drums, flute or chanting in dream-current dance, and a woven or stitched version of sacred cloth marks is sometimes worn at ceremonies, never tattooed or drawn on the skin. Clan memory songs record severed threads as warnings. See Nẹránamün and sacred arts and symbols.
