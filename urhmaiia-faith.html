Gods and faith

The Urhmaiia name the same divine balance as Nẹránamün in the Water-Echo tongue. This is the Urhmauuri pantheon, the origin myth and the words of belief.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Urhmaiia Canon Reference. Tags mark anything less settled. Terms are given in the keyboard romanisation, with the uvular letter written rh.

Lera and zekha. Both mean earth. Lera is earth as land, nature, terrain and the planet as a force. Zekha is the ground underfoot: floor, soil, dirt. A temple built into the lera is built into the natural world. A temple with a zekha floor just has a dirt floor.

Animal spirits

Urhaha and the Ruua

Titles
The Chooser of Tides, Sacred Voice of Water, Guide of the Deep Breath.
Role
Divine parent of the Urhmaiia. Gives resonance and form. Patron of change, cycles and memory.
Shows itself as
Tide-song, whirlpools and glowing resonance in Maubuʁ. Appears in dreams to Ezilii children.
Epithets
Aldarhuha, “Shifting Urhaha,” invoked for change. Amairhuha, “Calm Urhaha,” invoked for comfort or protection.
Tone
Willing and close. Invoked like an elder, remembered and spoken with, not an abstract force.

Ruua is not a god. It is the breath or resonance that runs through everything Urhaha touches. “Ruua flows in you” is a blessing. “The Ruua has dimmed” means someone is dying or in spiritual crisis. “Ruua-sent” describes a person acting with divine purpose. When an Urhmaiia dies, their essence goes back into the Ruua and is remembered in Maubuʁ. Death and memory belong to the Ruua itself, not to Maubuʁ as a land of the dead, though the Ruua is thickest there and ancestral memory is strongest for those who live in it.

Urhaha did not make the ocean. They gave it direction, form and invitation. “Urhaha was not the sea. They were its mind, its song, its choosing.”

The origin myth

“We came not from one sea, but from every flood the world tried to forget.”

Before the world had names there was breath, and the breath was Ruua: not wind but resonance, a deep vibration from which all water draws memory. From it came Urhaha, the Chooser of Tides, recognised by the Ruua as its will. Urhaha was the first to listen.

When the land cracked and the sky bled ice, the people scattered. Some stood still, sang to the soil and were buried. Others turned to the water and wept, not from weakness but as an invitation. Urhaha answered. From flooded canyon mouths and the bones of drowned forests, Urhaha shaped the first Urhmaiia, to keep a promise that no song sent to the sea would go unheard.

CasteHow the myth says they came to be
UrhmauukaThe firstborn: resonance given form, with eyes for light, lungs for breath and skin for tide. They were Ezilii at birth, whole and fluid, and carried the Ruua in their marrow.
UrhmateLate prayers, exiled children and swallowed caravans. Some were reshaped through grief, others through blood rites whispered by those who had lost Urhaha’s voice. Water-made and shadowed by separation, but no less real.
UrhmakeThe called back. They wake long after the old seas have drawn back, born human with the Ruua stirring in them like a second heart. They do not remember the sea, but the sea remembers them.

“To be Urhmaiia is not to be born. It is to be answered.”

Six names for the faith
