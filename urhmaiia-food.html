Food and craft

The oldest rule in Urhmaiia food is to speak to what you eat. The newest is about who has to.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Urhmaiia Canon Reference. Tags mark anything less settled. Terms are given in the keyboard romanisation, with the uvular letter written rh.

Amairuua

Amairuua is a brief ritual of acknowledgment before eating any living thing, spoken or telepathic. It recognises the creature’s life and returns its Ruua to the current. It is relational ethics, not prayer, and it is the oldest practice in the culture. What has split is who still does it, and why.

GroupPractice
Upper-tier UrhmauukaStill observed, but calcified into display. They eat everything, and some deliberately eat the fish that Urhmate are melded with.
Lower-tier UrhmauukaMostly dropped for practical reasons. They eat what there is, and upper tiers look down on this.
Traditional UrhmateKeep Amairuua and refuse their own melded species, since eating it collapses the boundary between self and food. Some refuse only the exact species, some related species, some all fish.
Young UrhmateTwo roads. One is full-practice revival: eat everything with real Amairuua, as a statement that fish heritage is not shameful. The other is invertebrates and plants only, as kinship with fish.

Esu is the word for any non-Urhmaiia sea creature that anyone may eat. Garesu is the Urhmate-only word for their own species-line, who are kin and never food. Azoiesu is the Urhmauuka term for the status-coded act of eating a species an Urhmate is melded with.

What they eat

FoodNotes
Cultivated seaweed and kelpThe most democratic food. A staple for every class.
Coral-farm produceA mid-tier staple.
InvertebratesClams, cephalopods, crustaceans, urchins and sea cucumbers. Even “vegetarian” Urhmate eat them.
FishThe common protein, with the debates above.
Large marine mammalsOccasional, and ceremonial among the traditional. More common in lean seasons.
Vent organismsTube worms, vent crabs and other extremophiles. A deep-water delicacy.
Glowing organismsSome are edible, and their glow transfers briefly to the eater. Used at Haza and mating ceremonies.
DolphinNot food. Dolphins are companions, and eating one would be like eating family.

Cooking without fire

There is no fire underwater, so every method is either geothermal (real vent heat, up to about 700°F at black smoker outflows) or chemical (acid, enzyme or fermentation).

DishMethod
TsoraifilaPrecision-sliced raw fish. The usual special-occasion home dish.
BorufilaRaw fish or invertebrate wrapped in cured kelp.
ZorufilaCooked or dried at a vent. Food in vessels at the outflow bakes, and strips hung in the superheated mineral air dry to jerky. A deep-water delicacy with a mineral flavour.
ZaifilaFish “cooked” by acid marination in mineral-pool brine, like ceviche.
AmaifilaCold-fermented seafood sealed in shell or coral vessels. The everyday counterpart to Zoruamai.
IzilofilaGlowing organisms prepared so the glow transfers. Luxury and ceremonial.

Food has to hold together in open water, so loose soups do not exist. Borubeka, an alginate paste, binds cooked fish, and Boruzhila, an edible kelp leaf, wraps it into something that can be bitten cleanly. Cooked food on land overwhelms first-timers, because the browning reaction makes flavours that have no underwater equivalent.

Amaiurhzakha, September to November, is the lean season. Elites draw on stored reserves while lower-tier Urhmauuka and most Urhmate compete for common food, and traditional abstentions get harder to keep.

Drinks and intoxicants

Urhmaiia take in water through food, so there is no drinking culture in the land sense. Still, there is a social structure of three tiers:

TierSubstanceWhat it is
CasualBoruloraKelp pods injected with Luaruua nutrient sap and crushed against the roof of the mouth.
IntoxicantZoruamaiA glowing, warming gel brewed by a Zoruii (vent-brewer) from sulfur bacteria and sweet seagrass roots, fermented in sealed vessels at a vent.
PsychedelicKirutokha, Vohutokha, or IziloaDiluted venom from the Spine Walker or the Spined Puffer, or Iziloa, a cultivated hallucinogenic microalga.

The social centre is the Vent Lounge: a gathering place around a vent or gas seep where one substance is passed from hand to hand, or mouth to gill, through a group. A Zoruii’s whole living depends on secure access to a vent. The syringe they use is a cured swim bladder fitted with a sharpened bone nozzle.
