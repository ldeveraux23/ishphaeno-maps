Appearance and dress

An Urhmaiia on land is read as a Black or brown person of Austronesian, Polynesian or Sub-Saharan African descent. The sea shows second.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Urhmaiia Canon Reference. Tags mark anything less settled. Terms are given in the keyboard romanisation, with the uvular letter written rh.

Faces

Three inspiration groups appear singly and mixed throughout the species, which is ancient and widespread.

Urhmauuka keep a full melanin baseline. The sea shows in a shimmer when wet, slightly wide pupils, a third eyelid, pointed or webbed ear edges, and neck gills that close flat on land.

Urhmate share the same baseline, with a second layer from the fish species they are melded with:

An Urhmate is never simply “green.” They are a dark-skinned person whose body has partly transformed.

Deep-water lineages

  • Urhmauuka: blue-black to indigo skin, very large pupils, faint natural glow along the jaw, collarbones and spine, and hair of deep black with a blue sheen.
  • Urhmate: melded with anglerfish, viperfish, barreleye or lanternfish. Glow is stronger and pulses with emotion. Some have translucent patches, and some have longer, more numerous teeth.

Hair

Every texture occurs. Underwater, hair fans and floats weightlessly. In deep lineages it can hold glowing particles. In Urhmate with strong fish melding it can take on scale colour, and unusual shades such as navy or green-tinted are typical of strong melding or deep-water lineage.

Ceremonial tattoos

Only Urhmauuka who have graduated from magic training or shown mastery of Ruala wear them. They glow, and three rules govern them:

  • Colour complements skin: warm gold on blue-black, cool silver-white or deep copper on rich dark brown, jewel tones such as teal and cobalt on mid-brown.
  • Intensity grows with power. New graduates have fine lines that barely glow. Masters have broad lines. The oldest glow in daylight.
  • Pattern follows the Ruala: flowing waves for water, branching Lichtenberg figures for electricity, scale geometry for fish command, and the most elaborate patterns of all for invisibility, which vanish with the caster.

Albinism and pale complexion

True albinism is genetic and occurs in either caste. Albino Urhmauuka are exceptionally rare, and reactions range from reverence to suspicion. In deep cities, where glow is the main visual language, their lack of pigment makes their own markings unusually vivid. A pale Urhmaiia without albinism is almost always an Urhmate whose fish is a light species, or an Urhmake of mixed descent. The dark skin is still underneath.

Inheritance among Urhmate

  • Parents of the same type pass on their species’ traits predictably, with small differences.
  • Parents of mixed types pass on the dominant parent’s traits most strongly, with blended or mosaic results. Some traits compete rather than blend.
  • Asymmetry, such as one arm scaled like one parent and the other like the other, is not unusual or alarming.

Garments

Urhmaiia dress for adornment, not cover, because loose fabric is no use in the water. Wardrobe is androgynous. Gender among the Urhmaiia is fluid and exists for mating, not for presentation, so nothing about dress is gendered. Status shows in material, craft and body modification. A loincloth, Borumora, is universal and worn by everyone. Layers are added by means and occasion. Common materials are seagrass and kelp, then Boruza (woven Blue Hypnea), and at the top Pezaraga, woven sea silk. Gold, Azoilira, is reserved for the Ajoiru royal line and ceremonial stewards, and is never functional because gold is too soft.

Ears are cartilage and fold flat against the skull in fast swimming or deep pressure. Most everyday clothing is biodegradable and nothing piles up in a home. Treated leather, reforged metal and status materials are the deliberate exceptions, kept and passed down precisely because permanence is rare.
