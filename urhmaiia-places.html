Cities and the deep

Urhmaiia civilisation lives in Earth’s trenches. The oldest and holiest part lies in Maubuʁ, a sacred ocean no human has mapped.

Lines without a tag are settled canon from the Urhmaiia Canon Reference. Tags mark anything less settled. Terms are given in the keyboard romanisation, with the uvular letter written rh.

Maubuʁ

Maubuʁ is a real, physical, sacred ocean, not an afterlife. It is the deep network of trenches in which most established Urhmaiia civilisation has always lived. Humanity has mapped only about a fifth of the ocean floor at useful resolution, and the great trenches (Challenger Deep, the Tonga Trench, the Philippine Trench) remain largely unmapped in fine detail. The Ruua current runs strongest and most undisturbed here because pollution and extraction have never touched it. That affects health, lifespan, Ruala strength and the quality of Haza.

Fringe-city Urhmaiia who visit for the first time say the Ruua is overwhelming, like breathing fully after a lifetime of thin air. The edge of Maubuʁ is called the Urhkatea, felt as a pressure against the skin and gills, then a sudden release and a rush of current.

Adzuratunua and the Kitezori

Adzuratunua is split by the Urhkatea. The Maubuʁ side holds the wealthiest old families and gate authorities. A checkpoint wall, the Kitezori, controls entry from the fringe, and its official purpose is sacred protection of Maubuʁ. The outer city is open, Urhmate-heavy and working class.

Travel and messages

NetworkWhat it is
Toritunua, the current roadsThe ocean’s deep circulation, supercharged by Ruua. Basin crossings take days, not months. Knowledge is passed down in families and deliberately not taught. Finding one as a child is an Urhaha-given sign.
Tunya, lava-tube transitAncient tubes between basins. Fast and dangerous. Needs a guide who knows the route, or military-grade Ruala. Tube mouths carry standard markers, though some are outdated as tubes shift.
Sofa, the message currentThe deep sound channel. Haulorai sent into it at the right depth crosses an entire basin as a slightly reverbed, flattened echo. Kautsorii descend to Sofa depth on rotation, listen, transcribe onto shell or basalt, and carry messages. They are almost always Urhmate.
Historical markersBasalt, volcanic rock or carved coral signs at tube mouths, city edges and ancient Ruuanakha. The oldest are in Maubuʁ. Some are not yet translated.

Government and vents

Each city-state was governed by a steward of Ajoiru royal blood, tasked with balancing Urhmauuka and Urhmate in their trench. The whole realm under the royal crown is called Ejasantunua. Hydrothermal vents are among the most contested resources, a bit like dams: whoever holds a vent holds energy, forging and the intoxicant supply. Zarehurua’s vent was never fully brought under a single faction.

Dry chambers

Dry air pockets (anchialine chambers) are real geological features. Some are trapped air in sloped limestone caves flooded as seas rose. Others are volcanic gas pockets near vents, and some of those are toxic. Wealthy households build artificial ones by carving a reversed diving-bell dome and holding the waterline down. A private dry room is a major status symbol.

Surfacing into one is a physical event: the neck gills collapse and flare, the third eyelid snaps back, skin sheds water in an instant, the muscles that push against ocean pressure suddenly relax, and the constant hum of the sea is replaced by sharp silence. Because telepathy is how Urhmaiia speak underwater, a dry room is where they speak aloud. Speaking with breath is intimate, private or formal, so the respectful and royal registers are most often spoken here.

Architecture

Urhmaiia cities are grown. Glyphs can be carved into young, soft coral, so they harden and rise as the coral matures. Older buildings have deeper, more weathered inscriptions and new wings have crisp ones. The Urhtsorna script groups letters by manner of articulation, and the idea is that building types might be marked by their dominant glyph family: stops for administration, r and rh for water-management structures, l and lh for temples and natal centres. Proposed Real-world sources to adapt: Bamum script from Cameroon, invented under King Njoya, and Adinkra symbols from the Akan of Ghana, which are carved into buildings as meaning-bearing decoration.
